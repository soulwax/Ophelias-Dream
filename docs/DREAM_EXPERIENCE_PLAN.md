# The dream: a playable prologue

The current atmosphere direction is specified in [Dream atmosphere without staged memory scenes](DREAM_ATMOSPHERE_PLAN.md). The live dream uses route-cued story events, six answers, four conversation rounds, and three outcomes. This original design below retains earlier mechanics for reference.

## Intent

The dream begins with ordinary winter unease and slowly approaches the psychological terror of living after sexual violence: hypervigilance, freezing, guarded space, broken recall and difficulty speaking. The player first shares small talk, then offers distance, time and a way back before the shadow chooses whether to say more. The assault itself is never shown. In the authorial story, a lumberjack raped Mathilda and she remains traumatized; the dream's last image warns that he is still nearby. Mathilda apologizes for being vague, never for surviving. The woman in the snow stays an indistinct female shadow; what she is to the dreamer remains unresolved.

This is a standalone game phase, selected beside Ophelia's and Mathilda's chapters. It lasts about **7–9 minutes on a first visit** and **4–5 minutes on replay**. The player walks, looks, pauses and makes one small physical choice before a subtitle choice. There is no spoken dialogue, fail state, chase timer, collectible count, required reading or moral score. Music stops on entering the phase. The existing `user://dream.cfg` memory persists across launches. Walking, stopping, looking back, shielding the lantern, passing it and returning all receive legible responses; none can strand the player.

The dream's purpose is to let players experience the story's central conflict before they hear either woman's account. Completing it should make the house lantern, two cups, footprints and the lake feel personal in the later chapters. It must not explain the story's contradictions or decide either chapter's ending.

### Current implementation to preserve or replace deliberately

`scripts/world/dream_experience.gd` starts beside the house, uses the CC0 `animated_woman_a.glb`, renders a silhouette and localized optical overlay, moves the figure along route offsets, offers an interact-or-wait lantern action, saves a four-way memory, and returns to the mode menu. Each landmark monologue stages a brief two-character camera shot with a speaker nameplate; the closing conversation holds that composition while the player and Mathilda exchange fixed subtitle turns and choices. The dark, gold-trimmed dialogue HUD keeps the world visible behind the exchange. The standalone ending keeps one image; Ophelia and Mathilda echoes are deferred to contextual moments in their own chapters by `scripts/ui/dream_reflection.gd`. Preserve the rig, saved IDs and working menu/phase flow.

The current route uses the existing terrain and reaches its answer on the first stretch of trail. `scripts/world/dream_route.gd` owns ground-aligned step, lantern, clearing and answer anchors, plus a terrain-following ice patch with soft edges, mottled depth and fine fractures. Four soft shadow pools sit beside the line of travel as quiet intervals; the snow path remains continuous and readable. A brief world-space shadow link draws across the snow during the final merge cue and recedes after the answer. The clearing still needs surrounding composition, a stronger house-light anomaly, and a fuller player-and-figure shadow treatment; distinct pass-and-return lantern responses and an event-specific dream soundscape also remain. `scripts/main.gd` still constructs the normal terrain, house, camp, lake, weather, soundscape and HUD in dream mode; explicitly gate chapter-only cues where they leak into this phase. The menu already stops its music on mode transition; keep that behavior for every mode.

`assets/dialogue/dream.json` is version 1. `scripts/world/dream_story.gd` and `tools/studio/dream_story.py` both validate it, while the latter drops unknown keys during normalization. Story Studio must learn any new field before an editor save. `Game.save_dream_memory()` and `Game.complete_dream()` own `user://dream.cfg`; do not create a second save. The existing narrative sources are `docs/MATHILDA_STORY.md`, `docs/MATHILDA_POV.md` and `docs/LAKE_MEETING.md`. Their central contradictions remain unresolved.

## Playable sequence

| Beat | What the player does | What the world reveals | Emotional turn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. The step** (about 1 minute) | Start outside the cabin; look back or run toward the treeline. | At the cut trees, Mathilda's first monologue turns a tree into a house and the axe into a question of permission. | Unease takes on a human shape without explaining itself. |
| **2. The distance** (about 2 minutes) | Follow at a chosen pace. Running never drains stamina. The figure walks rather than jumping ahead; if the player stops, she eventually stops too. | Near the delayed footstep, the Fates divide the road into leaving, following and freezing. A soft dark pool interrupts the snow between each spoken landmark. | Hypervigilance makes ordinary space feel unreliable. |
| **3. The light** (about 2 minutes) | Find a lantern turned toward the woods. The player may shelter it or keep moving; nobody asks why. | By the lantern, a Prometheus image tangles theft, fire and silence. Small talk wanders through Hestia, Ariadne and the forest before Mathilda apologizes for speaking in mazes and asks to continue. | Trust grows through consent and room to stop. |
| **4. The clearing** (about 1–2 minutes) | Follow the same snow line into the clearing; the dark patches mark pauses but never block the route. | At the tracks and ice, an Orpheus monologue questions who gets blamed in a story. The player can then answer “I said no,” “I went still,” “I was trying to get away,” or “I can't say it yet.” | The dream names trauma without showing the assault. |
| **5. The warning** (about 1 minute) | Hear her response, then choose whether to wake or stay with the closing image. | In her final caption, Mathilda apologizes for being vague and warns that the one who cut the trees is still out there; she saw his axe by the path. | The past is not safely over. The warning does not ask the player to become her rescuer. |

The figure should have continuous, readable locomotion and a small repertoire of gestures (pause, turn a shoulder, lower a hand, begin to reach, withdraw). Use the existing rig and animation library; retarget or blend a few short gestures if needed. Allow a single impossible relocation only when the figure is fully occluded by trees or snow. Never advance the scene because a distance threshold was crossed while the player was looking elsewhere. She should slow if the player lingers and should not sprint away if the player hurries. Match root travel to her walking pose; if a gesture animation is unavailable, use a restrained procedural pose rather than a mismatched clip.

### Route and interaction rules

Build the short route around the existing house and first stretch of trail, using `Trail.frame_at()` and `Trail.on_ground()` for anchors so an authored route shift or a different seed does not break it. The lantern sits visibly off the walking line, making the detour a choice. The clearing is a small composed pocket with dark reflective ground; if the distant game lake cannot be seen from the first 70 metres, imply ice locally instead of depending on an impossible sightline. Keep the route readable on lean graphics through light, trunks and tracks alone.

Use an ordered state machine: `STEP → DISTANCE → LIGHT → CLEARING → SMALL TALK → DISCLOSURE → WARNING → WAKE`. A spatial region plus its associated event advances each route beat. Events are idempotent: backtracking neither replays captions nor creates another figure. At the lantern, `interact` can shield the flame, waiting beside it can steady it, and passing it remains valid. Returning after passing gives a third response until the player reaches the clearing. The flame and wind show what happened even when prompts are disabled. Both approaches join at the same clearing without a hidden score. At the clearing, the player gets two short subtitle turns with ordinary conversational openings and gentle answers. They can offer quiet, time, distance or a way back. The dream never requires disclosure; choosing not to speak is a complete answer.

The four current beat IDs map to route progress, not figure proximity: `tree_line` and `footsteps` fire along the trail, `lantern` fires near the lantern, and `empty_path` leads into the conversation. `opening` is shown at the step and `arrival` is the quiet setup before small talk. Trigger each at most once. If the player misses a region by walking around it, crossing its forward progress line still advances the event. Choice focus must be clear on keyboard, mouse and controller. Keep the world and camera visible while subtitle choices appear; the player can pause and answer in the snow rather than entering a separate terminal menu. After the remembered response, the final subtitle carries Mathilda's apology and warning; standalone mode holds it on the closing card.

The house light remains an orientation anchor if the player turns back. After an extended lack of progress, an optional unobtrusive reorientation caption may appear if prompts are enabled; navigation cannot depend on that line. Never invert input, move the player's body, force a camera turn, or require a timed button press. Returning toward the house before the conversation leads back to the step and does not reset the dream. After choosing an answer, the player can read Mathilda's apology and warning before waking; standalone mode keeps both visible on the closing card.

## Choice and consequences

After small talk, ask **“What do you wish someone understood?”** with no speaker label or voice. Four responses name different experiences without ranking them or requiring more detail:

| Existing memory ID | Proposed answer | Dream response | Later echo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `name` | “I said no.” | The figure keeps her eyes on yours; the word stays present. | Remembering that no is complete. |
| `waiting` | “I went still. That wasn't yes.” | She lowers her hands; nothing is asked of the player. | Reclaiming choice and bodily autonomy. |
| `remember` | “I was trying to get away.” | The tracks turn toward the house, by choice. | The path home belongs to the player. |
| `silence` | “I can't say it yet.” | She waits at the same distance. | A pause is a valid answer. |

Keep these IDs so existing saves retain a valid memory. Story Studio and both normalizers must preserve the optional small-talk exchange (up to three turns) and final `ending` line. The warning identifies the lumberjack through the cut trees and his axe only at the end; it does not depict the rape or imply that the player must confront him. Keep separate Ophelia and Mathilda echoes tied to each woman's circumstances and a concrete object or place. Trigger each once after chapter start without overlapping a voiced line, tutorial prompt or conversation. The memory changes perception, not journal requirements, encounter odds or ending eligibility.

Treat dream captions as perceptions, not objective exposition. Avoid naming either woman in the standalone phase, labeling the shadow's speech, or saying “she was you.” Keep a short opening control hint separate from the story and reflect remapped input. Queue the four landmark monologues in route order and hold each long enough to read at the largest subtitle size; leave a brief silent interval before the next. If subtitles are disabled, preserve necessary control/choice text and communicate the route through the world. The final script should be read against movement and silence; remove lines that explain their own symbols.

## Image and sound direction

### Dreamscape unrealism — major visual subtask

The dream follows a rule of **local truth and global impossibility**. Any single patch of snow, doorway, path segment or cluster of trees should look physically plausible when viewed nearby. Contradictions emerge only when the player relates two or more places: a route occupies more distance than the landscape can contain, a familiar light remains visible from the wrong side of a hill, or a path bends back above the ground without an obvious transition. The world is malfunctioning coherently rather than collapsing into surreal clutter.

Use a small anomaly budget: one persistent spatial contradiction per composition and no more than one short optical accent at the same time. Preserve ordinary snow, trees and architecture around each anomaly so the eye has a stable reference. Prefer slow discovery through movement and parallax over sudden geometry changes. Nothing impossible may block the playable route, resemble a required objective, invert controls, change collision beneath the player or make the figure unreadable.

The art-directable motif set is:

| Motif | Local reading | Global contradiction | Reveal rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Folded path** | A narrow snow-covered continuation beside the clearing. | Its distant section rises, turns back over its own footprint and ends above a nearer point. | The grounded beginning reads first; elevation and reverse direction emerge through parallax after the lantern. |
| **Wrong horizon light** | A warm house light seen through trees. | It remains visible from terrain that should fully occlude the house and appears at a subtly incorrect bearing. | Keep it small and steady; let player movement expose the bad relationship. |
| **Repeated threshold** | An ordinary frame, pair of posts or tree gap. | The same proportions recur at incompatible scales and distances. | Limit repetition to two or three instances and avoid a decorative rhythm. |
| **Layered clearing** | Snow, dark ice and a pale horizon form a readable basin. | Reflections or shadows imply a second orientation of the same space. | Reserve this for the boundary/answer beat; keep bodies and controls anchored to the real ground. |

Art direction should expose semantic controls such as fold height, return distance, lateral offset, reveal amount, haze and repetition count. Full and lean graphics use the same geometry and composition; full graphics may add fog integration and restrained edge breakup. Screen-effects reduction removes optical doubling but never removes the spatial contradiction itself.

The first implementation is the **folded path** owned by `scripts/world/dream_route.gd` with a dream-only material. It starts outside the walking line, conforms to terrain long enough to be mistaken for a second route, then lifts and doubles back without collision. Later passes may add the wrong horizon light and repeated threshold only after this motif is reviewed in motion; do not stack them merely to increase spectacle.

Give the phase three strong compositions rather than a uniform filter:

1. **Warm point / blue field.** The cabin window and lantern are the only amber anchors in a blue-black snowfield. Snow is fine and slow near the camera; the distant forest remains dark enough to silhouette the figure. Reuse the cabin, lantern, tree, ground and snow assets already in the project.
2. **The path folds.** As the player's own footfall arrives ahead, a few flakes hang or drift upward for less than a second. A light in the distance appears through an impossible gap in the trees. Let the anomaly occur in the world, then settle; constant screen distortion would make it ordinary.
3. **The clearing opens.** Thin the snow and wind, reveal dark ice and its reflection, and frame the small shadow against the pale horizon. The figure never becomes a portrait. For a few seconds, the player's outline and hers share the same slow edge breakup while a shadow-like projection travels between them. The bodies remain separate and controllable. Reserve the strongest warp pulse for the choice and wake, then let the frame settle sharply.

Use `shaders/dream_shadow.gdshader` on the figure, `shaders/dream_warp.gdshader` as a local, brief optical disturbance near her, and `shaders/dream_shadow_link.gdshader` for the temporary world-space shadow link. Improve the silhouette with light and motion before increasing shader intensity. Tie effect strength to events and to `Settings.screen_effects`; provide a nearly static reduced-effects path. Keep captions on a darkening scrim only when contrast needs it, within a safe width at 16:9 and narrower windows. Use the player's subtitle size setting and never put essential text solely in a fading world projection.

The sound arc begins with ordinary snow steps, wind and a cabin window tone. At most two second, offset footfalls should be spatial and separated by silence; do not loop them. The figure should have almost no signature sound. The current figure-attached `howl.ogg` should become environmental wind or be removed so she does not sound supernatural by default. At the lantern, wind shields or opens with the player's position. In the clearing, remove most layers for one near-silent interval so the ice and a soft fabric/wood sound can be heard. Leave another short silence after the choice. Build from existing weather, step, tree-creak and house assets after checking their provenance and fit. Do not use speech, reversed speech, a generic horror sting or menu music. Route levels through the game's audio buses and respect mute and volume settings.

### Psychedelic and ego-dissolving shading

The visual progression is perceptual: ordinary materials and spatial relationships briefly disobey expectation. Each effect has a trigger, a short duration, a story reason and a reduced-effects equivalent. The player must always be able to see the path, player body and figure.

| Cue | Full treatment | Reduced-effects / lean treatment | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Doubt, at the step** | Window warmth appears faintly in snow at an impossible angle; the second track appears after occlusion. | Static warm bounce and delayed track reveal. | No geometry popping in direct view. |
| **Anticipation, on the path** | Local flakes hesitate or drift upward for less than a second with the displaced footstep; a tree gap has a faint doubled edge. | The footstep and a slight snow-light pulse. | No navigation-wide wobble. |
| **Care, at the lantern** | Flame, glass and nearby snow respond together; shelter lets the figure's rim catch a little amber, while passing leaves blue dominant. | Light and figure posture carry the difference. | No good/bad color grade. |
| **Boundary, in the clearing** | A dark projection from the player's feet approaches the figure. For roughly 3–6 seconds, shared world-space noise gently fragments both outlines; a delayed edge makes one movement seem to belong to both. Solid cores remain legible. | Two converging ground projections and a synchronized gesture, with no distortion. | No player invisibility, collision change or uncertain camera aim. |
| **Release, at answer/wake** | One brief local optical pulse around the figure, then a crisp house light and dark surface. | Light falloff and sound dropout only. | No flashes, strobe or repeating color cycle. |

Use the existing `dream_shadow.gdshader` as a depth-tested anonymous silhouette. Tune its rim and opacity against the actual dream lighting before adding noise. The existing mesh has shadow casting disabled, so the meeting shadows need a separate projected mesh/decal or simple ground geometry; do not assume real-time shadows will merge. Shared **world-space** noise coordinates and a slowly changing phase can connect the temporary player-edge treatment to the figure without permanently replacing the player's materials. Prefer dithered edge breakup over large transparent areas, which can sort badly against snow and hair. If a clean player-edge pass proves invasive, use only the ground projections and synchronized gesture.

`dream_warp.gdshader` currently draws a translucent canvas overlay. Use it as a brief, local accent around the figure, with strength driven by named cues and multiplied by `Game.settings.screen_effects`. Zero must mean no distortion. When the figure is behind the camera or off screen, suppress the effect; clamping a projected point to the screen edge would create a false pulse. Put captions and controls above the effect layer. Prefer restrained chromatic separation and temporal echo to generic scanlines or constant glitch. Keep three strong compositions visible in a contact sheet: amber cabin in a blue field, off-axis lantern with two human positions, and pale clearing with black ice, small figure and impossible house light.

For `screen_effects = 0`, preserve composition, posture, shadow placement and sound while removing warp, doubled edges and dissolving contours. Lean graphics must retain the cabin anchor, tracks, lantern response and figure readability. No forced field-of-view swing, input lag or camera shake beyond the player's setting. Keep transparent snow, fog and particle layers sparse on the project's Intel Iris Xe target. Captions and choices must respect `Settings.SUBTITLE_SIZES`, `ui_scale`, and narrow windows; use a dark scrim only where contrast needs it.

### Runtime, data and failure contracts

- Keep schema version 1 and all four saved branch IDs. Preserve optional `small_talk` rounds (up to three, each with two to four short choices) and an `ending` line in both normalizers and Story Studio. Older version-1 files without these fields remain loadable with no small-talk rounds and `arrival` as the closing fallback. JSON owns words; runtime state owns timing.
- Dream scene nodes should be children of `dream_experience.gd` or small dream-only helpers. `scripts/main.gd` currently builds normal weather, wildlife, camp, lake, soundscape and HUD in dream mode; explicitly gate ordinary chapter cues and interactions without changing other modes. Voice, Encounters, chapter endings and the day clock must stay inactive in DREAM.
- `Game.save_dream_memory()` saves immediately after an answer; `Game.complete_dream()` marks completion only on wake. Exiting before choosing must not erase a prior answer. Replaying may replace the answer without changing chapter progress. Unknown or legacy saved IDs use the existing safe lookup behavior.
- Missing story data should log a clear error and return to a usable title state. Missing figure animation should fall back to a static posture without blocking progress. Missing shader or disabled effects should leave the complete route and choice playable. Spatial triggers need recovery if the player approaches from an unexpected angle.
- Compare frame time with dream cues on and off at the same step, lantern and clearing viewpoints in full and lean graphics. If the effect causes a sustained drop, trim transparent snow/fog overlap and overdraw before reducing silhouette readability. Keep a still image of each viewpoint plus a short moving capture; static screenshots alone cannot show timing, motion comfort or the merge.

## Acceptance criteria

- A first-time player can describe what they did in the dream, why the lantern mattered, and what their own answer meant, without needing an explanation of the plot. Following, stopping and returning all allow completion.
- The figure remains female in posture and movement but cannot be identified by face, outfit or voice.
- Stopping, following and returning are all supported; no timer or invisible trigger strands the player.
- The dream contains at least three distinct visual compositions and two deliberate moments of near-silence. Its psychedelic cues grow from physical anomalies to one brief ego-boundary event, then the world settles. Disabling screen effects preserves the same meaning and route.
- At least one spatial anomaly is plausible in a close view and impossible in a wider view. A player can discover the contradiction through ordinary movement and parallax; it does not depend on a caption, post effect, teleport or camera cut.
- No composition contains more than one dominant geometric contradiction. Repetition, impossible scale and folded space remain sparse enough that the house light, figure and playable path retain their hierarchy.
- All four memories produce a distinct closing response and an appropriate echo in both chapters, while the core story and endings remain ambiguous.
- The dream remains playable on lean graphics and on both authored and generated routes, subtitle-only, and fully navigable with keyboard, mouse and controller. Large text, remapped interaction, muted sound, pause, exit and replay work without corrupting the saved choice.

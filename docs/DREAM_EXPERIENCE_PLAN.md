# The dream: a playable prologue

## Intent

The dream asks a human question: **when someone leaves after an argument, are you following because you want to hear them, or because you cannot bear their absence?** The player should feel both impulses without being told which is virtuous. The woman in the snow stays an indistinct female shadow. Her identity, and whether she is separate from the dreamer, remain unresolved.

This is a standalone game phase, selected beside Ophelia's and Mathilda's chapters. It lasts about **7–9 minutes on a first visit** and **4–5 minutes on replay**. The player walks, looks, pauses and makes one small physical choice before a subtitle choice. There is no spoken dialogue, fail state, chase timer, collectible count, required reading or moral score. Music stops on entering the phase. The existing `user://dream.cfg` memory persists across launches. Walking, stopping, looking back, shielding the lantern, passing it and returning all receive legible responses; none can strand the player.

The dream's purpose is to let players experience the story's central conflict before they hear either woman's account. Completing it should make the house lantern, two cups, footprints and the lake feel personal in the later chapters. It must not explain the story's contradictions or decide either chapter's ending.

### Current implementation to preserve or replace deliberately

`scripts/world/dream_experience.gd` starts beside the house, uses the CC0 `animated_woman_a.glb`, renders a silhouette and localized optical overlay, moves the figure along route offsets, offers an interact-or-wait lantern action, saves a four-way memory, and returns to the mode menu. The subtitle choices remain in `Game.Phase.DREAM`, with the world visible behind them. The standalone ending keeps one image; Ophelia and Mathilda echoes are deferred to contextual moments in their own chapters by `scripts/ui/dream_reflection.gd`. Preserve the rig, saved IDs and working menu/phase flow.

The current route uses the existing terrain and reaches its answer on the first stretch of trail. `scripts/world/dream_route.gd` now owns ground-aligned step, lantern, clearing and answer anchors, plus a terrain-following ice patch with soft edges, mottled depth and fine fractures. A brief world-space shadow link draws across the snow during the final merge cue and recedes after the answer. The clearing still needs surrounding composition, a stronger house-light anomaly, and a fuller player-and-figure shadow treatment; distinct pass-and-return lantern responses and an event-specific dream soundscape also remain. `scripts/main.gd` still constructs the normal terrain, house, camp, lake, weather, soundscape and HUD in dream mode; explicitly gate chapter-only cues where they leak into this phase. The menu already stops its music on mode transition; keep that behavior for every mode.

`assets/dialogue/dream.json` is version 1. `scripts/world/dream_story.gd` and `tools/studio/dream_story.py` both validate it, while the latter drops unknown keys during normalization. Story Studio must learn any new field before an editor save. `Game.save_dream_memory()` and `Game.complete_dream()` own `user://dream.cfg`; do not create a second save. The existing narrative sources are `docs/MATHILDA_STORY.md`, `docs/MATHILDA_POV.md` and `docs/LAKE_MEETING.md`. Their central contradictions remain unresolved.

## Playable sequence

| Beat | What the player does | What the world reveals | Emotional turn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. The step** (about 1 minute) | Start outside the cabin; look back or walk toward the treeline. Two cups sit together on the step, the window lantern is on, and a woman-shaped absence stands beyond the first trees. | The house is a place someone could return to, not just a point of departure. One set of prints begins at the door; the second begins only when the player looks away. | Recognition and unease. |
| **2. The distance** (about 2 minutes) | Follow at a chosen pace. The figure walks rather than jumping ahead. If the player stops, she eventually stops too. The path is always legible from light, tree spacing and prints. | A step sounds ahead just after the player's own. Once, the prints appear to come back toward the house. The figure is sometimes close enough for an ordinary human gesture, never for a clear face. | Pursuit turns into uncertainty about who is being pursued. |
| **3. The light** (about 2 minutes) | Find a lantern losing its flame at the edge of the path. The player can shelter beside it for a few breaths, follow the figure past it, or return after passing. No button timing and no failure. | Staying near the light lets the figure approach of her own accord. Continuing toward her lets the player reach the same clearing from another angle. Either action has a distinct image and sound response, then rejoins the sequence. | The player can offer a place to return without forcing a return. |
| **4. The clearing** (about 1–2 minutes) | Enter a small, composed space overlooking dark ice. The cabin light is visible behind the player even though the route should have hidden it. Move close, hold back, or look at the two tracks. | The figure stands still. For one brief moment her shadow and the player's shadow meet; the bodies do not. The wind thins enough to hear the ice and a last footstep. | An answer seems possible, but possession of it is not. |
| **5. The answer** (about 1 minute) | At interaction range, choose one subtitle response while the world and camera remain live. The figure's reaction is a restrained motion, not a speech or reveal. Walk toward the house light or press interact to wake. | The choice names the player's reason for following, not the figure's identity. The final image holds long enough to read and remember. | The player leaves with a question about their own motive. |

The figure should have continuous, readable locomotion and a small repertoire of gestures (pause, turn a shoulder, lower a hand, begin to reach, withdraw). Use the existing rig and animation library; retarget or blend a few short gestures if needed. Allow a single impossible relocation only when the figure is fully occluded by trees or snow. Never advance the scene because a distance threshold was crossed while the player was looking elsewhere. She should slow if the player lingers and should not sprint away if the player hurries. Match root travel to her walking pose; if a gesture animation is unavailable, use a restrained procedural pose rather than a mismatched clip.

### Route and interaction rules

Build the short route around the existing house and first stretch of trail, using `Trail.frame_at()` and `Trail.on_ground()` for anchors so an authored route shift or a different seed does not break it. The lantern sits visibly off the walking line, making the detour a choice. The clearing is a small composed pocket with dark reflective ground; if the distant game lake cannot be seen from the first 70 metres, imply ice locally instead of depending on an impossible sightline. Keep the route readable on lean graphics through light, trunks and tracks alone.

Use an ordered state machine: `STEP → DISTANCE → LIGHT → CLEARING → ANSWER → WAKE`. A spatial region plus its associated event advances each beat. Events are idempotent: backtracking neither replays captions nor creates another figure. At the lantern, `interact` can shield the flame, waiting beside it can steady it, and passing it remains valid. Returning after passing gives a third response until the player reaches the clearing. The flame and wind show what happened even when prompts are disabled. Both approaches join at the same clearing without a hidden score.

The four current beat IDs map to route progress, not figure proximity: `tree_line` and `footsteps` fire along the trail, `lantern` fires near the lantern, and `empty_path` leads into the answer. `opening` is shown at the step and `arrival` before the answer. Trigger each at most once. If the player misses a region by walking around it, crossing its forward progress line still advances the event. Choice focus must be clear on keyboard, mouse and controller. Keep the world and camera visible while the subtitle choices appear in the dream phase; movement currently remains available, so the player can answer without being stopped at a fixed spot. This should feel like answering in the snow, not leaving the dream for a terminal menu.

The house light remains an orientation anchor if the player turns back. After an extended lack of progress, an optional unobtrusive reorientation caption may appear if prompts are enabled; navigation cannot depend on that line. Never invert input, move the player's body, force a camera turn, or require a timed button press. Returning toward the house before answering leads back to the step and does not reset the dream. After answering, `interact` always wakes the player even if they cannot find the house light.

## Choice and consequences

Replace the current question, **“Before you wake, what do you ask her?”**, with a question about the player's motive: **“Why did you come after me?”** It appears without a speaker label and without a voice; the player may read it as the figure's question or their own. Four responses should represent distinct, understandable impulses, with no approval or punishment:

| Existing memory ID | Proposed answer | Dream response | Later echo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `name` | “I wanted to know who I was following.” | The figure turns enough to suggest recognition, but the face stays in shadow. | A name feels familiar before it is spoken. |
| `waiting` | “I thought you might want me to wait.” | She takes one small step nearer, then lets the space remain. | The lit window or paired cups briefly recall a place kept for someone. |
| `remember` | “I was afraid you would not come back.” | The player's prints are visible beside hers, then snow softens both. | A later absence brings back the fear, without telling the player what happened. |
| `silence` | Say nothing. | She looks toward the house instead of at the player; its light remains on regardless of the lantern choice. | The lack of an answer is remembered as a choice, not an empty branch. |

Keep these IDs so existing saves retain a valid memory. Write separate Ophelia and Mathilda echoes in `assets/dialogue/dream.json`; each should reflect that woman's circumstances and use one concrete object or place. In Ophelia's chapter, the echo belongs near the house step or first bend; in Mathilda's, near her camp and cups. Trigger it once after chapter start without overlapping a voiced line, tutorial prompt or conversation, and allow a quick-moving player to receive it. The standalone dream should show only a short closing image and the chosen line, then return to the mode menu. Do not display both women's future echoes together immediately after the dream; that tells the player how to interpret it before playing either story. The memory changes perception, not journal requirements, encounter odds or ending eligibility.

Treat dream captions as perceptions, not objective exposition. Avoid naming either woman in the standalone phase, labeling the shadow's speech, or saying “she was you.” Keep a short opening control hint separate from the story and reflect remapped input. Budget one short line at the step, one at the displaced footstep, one at the lantern, one at the clearing, then the question and answer. Do not replace a line before it can be read at the largest subtitle size. If subtitles are disabled, preserve necessary control/choice text and communicate the route through the world. The final script should be read against movement and silence; remove lines that explain their own symbols.

## Image and sound direction

Give the phase three strong compositions rather than a uniform filter:

1. **Warm point / blue field.** The cabin window and lantern are the only amber anchors in a blue-black snowfield. Snow is fine and slow near the camera; the distant forest remains dark enough to silhouette the figure. Reuse the cabin, lantern, tree, ground and snow assets already in the project.
2. **The path folds.** As the player's own footfall arrives ahead, a few flakes hang or drift upward for less than a second. A light in the distance appears through an impossible gap in the trees. Let the anomaly occur in the world, then settle; constant screen distortion would make it ordinary.
3. **The clearing opens.** Thin the snow and wind, reveal dark ice and its reflection, and frame the small shadow against the pale horizon. The figure never becomes a portrait. For a few seconds, the player's outline and hers share the same slow edge breakup while a shadow-like projection travels between them. The bodies remain separate and controllable. Reserve the strongest warp pulse for the choice and wake, then let the frame settle sharply.

Use `shaders/dream_shadow.gdshader` on the figure, `shaders/dream_warp.gdshader` as a local, brief optical disturbance near her, and `shaders/dream_shadow_link.gdshader` for the temporary world-space shadow link. Improve the silhouette with light and motion before increasing shader intensity. Tie effect strength to events and to `Settings.screen_effects`; provide a nearly static reduced-effects path. Keep captions on a darkening scrim only when contrast needs it, within a safe width at 16:9 and narrower windows. Use the player's subtitle size setting and never put essential text solely in a fading world projection.

The sound arc begins with ordinary snow steps, wind and a cabin window tone. At most two second, offset footfalls should be spatial and separated by silence; do not loop them. The figure should have almost no signature sound. The current figure-attached `howl.ogg` should become environmental wind or be removed so she does not sound supernatural by default. At the lantern, wind shields or opens with the player's position. In the clearing, remove most layers for one near-silent interval so the ice and a soft fabric/wood sound can be heard. Leave another short silence after the choice. Build from existing weather, step, tree-creak and house assets after checking their provenance and fit. Do not use speech, reversed speech, a generic horror sting or menu music. Route levels through the game's audio buses and respect mute and volume settings.

### Psychedelic and ego-dissolving shading

The visual progression is perceptual: ordinary materials and spatial relationships briefly disobey expectation. Each effect has a trigger, a short duration, a story reason and a reduced-effects equivalent. The player must always be able to see the path, player body and figure.

| Cue | Full treatment | Reduced-effects / lean treatment | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Doubt, at the step** | Window warmth appears faintly in snow at an impossible angle; the second track appears after occlusion. | Static warm bounce and delayed track reveal. | No geometry popping in direct view. |
| **Anticipation, on the path** | Local flakes hesitate or drift upward for less than a second with the displaced footstep; a tree gap has a faint doubled edge. | The footstep and a slight snow-light pulse. | No navigation-wide wobble. |
| **Care, at the lantern** | Flame, glass and nearby snow respond together; shelter lets the figure's rim catch a little amber, while passing leaves blue dominant. | Light and figure posture carry the difference. | No good/bad color grade. |
| **Boundary, in the clearing** | A dark projection from the player's feet approaches the figure. For roughly 3–6 seconds, shared world-space noise gently fragments both outlines; a delayed edge makes one movement seem to belong to both. Solid cores remain legible. | Two converging ground projections and a synchronized gesture, with no distortion. | No player invisibility, collision change or uncertain camera aim. |
| **Release, at answer/wake** | One brief local optical pulse around the figure, then a crisp house light and dark surface. | Light falloff and sound dropout only. | No flashes, strobe or repeating color cycle. |

Use the existing `dream_shadow.gdshader` as a depth-tested anonymous silhouette. Tune its rim and opacity against the actual dream lighting before adding noise. The existing mesh has shadow casting disabled, so the meeting shadows need a separate projected mesh/decal or simple ground geometry; do not assume real-time shadows will merge. Shared **world-space** noise coordinates and a slowly changing phase can connect the temporary player-edge treatment to the figure without permanently replacing the player's materials. Prefer dithered edge breakup over large transparent areas, which can sort badly against snow and hair. If a clean player-edge pass proves invasive, use only the ground projections and synchronized gesture.

`dream_warp.gdshader` currently draws a translucent canvas overlay. Use it as a brief, local accent around the figure, with strength driven by named cues and multiplied by `Game.settings.screen_effects`. Zero must mean no distortion. When the figure is behind the camera or off screen, suppress the effect; clamping a projected point to the screen edge would create a false pulse. Put captions and controls above the effect layer. Prefer restrained chromatic separation and temporal echo to generic scanlines or constant glitch. Keep three strong compositions visible in a contact sheet: amber cabin in a blue field, off-axis lantern with two human positions, and pale clearing with black ice, small figure and impossible house light.

For `screen_effects = 0`, preserve composition, posture, shadow placement and sound while removing warp, doubled edges and dissolving contours. Lean graphics must retain the cabin anchor, tracks, lantern response and figure readability. No forced field-of-view swing, input lag or camera shake beyond the player's setting. Keep transparent snow, fog and particle layers sparse on the project's Intel Iris Xe target. Captions and choices must respect `Settings.SUBTITLE_SIZES`, `ui_scale`, and narrow windows; use a dark scrim only where contrast needs it.

### Runtime, data and failure contracts

- Keep schema version 1 and its required fields. Add an optional `lantern_variants` object with exactly `shelter`, `pass` and `return` single-line captions, each under the existing 240-character beat limit. The new story should author all three; old version-1 files without the object remain loadable and fall back to the `lantern` beat. Update `scripts/world/dream_story.gd`, `tools/studio/dream_story.py`, Story Studio, and their existing probes together. The Python normalizer currently drops unknown fields. JSON owns words; runtime state owns timing.
- Dream scene nodes should be children of `dream_experience.gd` or small dream-only helpers. `scripts/main.gd` currently builds normal weather, wildlife, camp, lake, soundscape and HUD in dream mode; explicitly gate ordinary chapter cues and interactions without changing other modes. Voice, Encounters, chapter endings and the day clock must stay inactive in DREAM.
- `Game.save_dream_memory()` saves immediately after an answer; `Game.complete_dream()` marks completion only on wake. Exiting before choosing must not erase a prior answer. Replaying may replace the answer without changing chapter progress. Unknown or legacy saved IDs use the existing safe lookup behavior.
- Missing story data should log a clear error and return to a usable title state. Missing figure animation should fall back to a static posture without blocking progress. Missing shader or disabled effects should leave the complete route and choice playable. Spatial triggers need recovery if the player approaches from an unexpected angle.
- Compare frame time with dream cues on and off at the same step, lantern and clearing viewpoints in full and lean graphics. If the effect causes a sustained drop, trim transparent snow/fog overlap and overdraw before reducing silhouette readability. Keep a still image of each viewpoint plus a short moving capture; static screenshots alone cannot show timing, motion comfort or the merge.

## Acceptance criteria

- A first-time player can describe what they did in the dream, why the lantern mattered, and what their own answer meant, without needing an explanation of the plot. Following, stopping and returning all allow completion.
- The figure remains female in posture and movement but cannot be identified by face, outfit or voice.
- Stopping, following and returning are all supported; no timer or invisible trigger strands the player.
- The dream contains at least three distinct visual compositions and two deliberate moments of near-silence. Its psychedelic cues grow from physical anomalies to one brief ego-boundary event, then the world settles. Disabling screen effects preserves the same meaning and route.
- All four memories produce a distinct closing response and an appropriate echo in both chapters, while the core story and endings remain ambiguous.
- The dream remains playable on lean graphics and on both authored and generated routes, subtitle-only, and fully navigable with keyboard, mouse and controller. Large text, remapped interaction, muted sound, pause, exit and replay work without corrupting the saved choice.
